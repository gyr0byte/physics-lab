# Official Nepal CDC Curriculum Reference — Physics Lab

## 1. Institutional Context

- **Application:** Physics Lab (Offline Educational Smart TV Application)
- **Institution:** Shree Public High School, Dharan-12, Sunsari, Koshi Province, Nepal
- **Target Audience:** Grade 11 and Grade 12 Secondary Science Students (National Examinations Board — NEB)
- **Primary Language:** English
- **Pedagogical Model:** Interactive, discovery-based virtual physics laboratory inspired by PhET principles, engineered natively for classroom Android TV (16:9 landscape, D-pad remote navigation) and laboratory touchscreens.

## 3. Curriculum Structure & Content Areas

The secondary physics curriculum in Nepal is systematically partitioned into **5 Content Areas** spanning both grades:

| Content Area | Grade 11 Chapters | Grade 12 Chapters | Total Chapters | Core Themes |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|---|
| **1. Mechanics** | 8 | 3 | 11 | Kinematics, dynamics, energy, gravitation, rotational dynamics, SHM, fluids |
| **2. Heat & Thermodynamics** | 5 | 2 | 7 | Thermal expansion, calorimetry, heat transfer, ideal gases, 1st & 2nd laws |
| **3. Waves and Optics** | 5 | 8 | 13 | Geometrical optics (Gr 11), Wave motion, sound, interference, diffraction, polarization (Gr 12) |
| **4. Electricity and Magnetism** | 5 | 6 | 11 | Electrostatics, capacitance, DC circuits (Gr 11), Kirchhoff, electromagnetism, AC (Gr 12) |
| **5. Modern Physics** | 3 | 6 | 9 | Nuclear physics, energy levels, solids (Gr 11), Electrons, photons, quantum, radioactivity, seismology (Gr 12) |
| **Total** | **26** | **25** | **51** | **Complete secondary physics scope** |

## 4. Academic Evaluation Frameworks & Teaching Hours

To ensure clear academic source traceability, three distinct regulatory frameworks are distinguished:

### A. CDC Curriculum Framework & Credit Hour Distribution
In the *Secondary Education Curriculum 2076 (Physics Subject Code Phy. 101 / Phy. 102)*, Physics is designated as a 5-credit-hour annual course with **160 total instructional working hours** per grade:
- **Theory Component (Phy. 101 / Phy. 102):** Explicitly allocated **4 credit hours = 128 annual working hours**.
- **Practical Component (Phy. 101 PR / Phy. 102 PR):** Explicitly allocated **1 credit hour = 32 annual laboratory working hours**.

### B. CDC External Examination Specification Grid (75 Theory Marks)
The external summative written examination is conducted by the National Examinations Board (NEB) at the end of each academic session:
- **Full Marks:** 75 Marks | **Time:** 3 Hours
- **Question Structure:**
  - Multiple Choice Questions (MCQ): $11 \times 1 = 11$ marks
  - Short Answer Questions (SAQ): $8 \times 5 = 40$ marks (with internal choice in selected items)
  - Long Answer Questions (LAQ): $3 \times 8 = 24$ marks (with internal choice in selected items)
- **Cognitive Domain Weightage:** Knowledge and Understanding (~30%), Application (~40%), Higher-Order Thinking Skills (~30%).

### C. NEB Internal Practical Evaluation Scheme (25 Practical Marks)
According to the official **NEB Physics Internal Practical Evaluation Scheme** (effective for NEB examination sessions including the 2026/2082 BS framework):
- **Classroom Participation & Attendance:** 3 marks
- **First Trimester Internal Examination (Practical):** 3 marks
- **Second Trimester Internal Examination (Practical):** 3 marks
- **Laboratory Experiment Performance:** 10 marks
- **Practical Notebook / Record Copy & Project Work:** 3 marks
- **Viva Voce (Oral Examination):** 3 marks
- **Total Practical Marks:** **25 Marks**

## 5. Level-Wise Subject Competencies vs. Application Educational Goals

To maintain absolute academic integrity, official statutory competencies defined by the Curriculum Development Centre are strictly separated from the application-specific synthesized design goals of Physics Lab:

### A. Official CDC Physics Competencies (Grades 11–12)
The *Secondary Education Curriculum 2076 (Section: Subject-Specific Competencies)* defines exactly **five level-wise competencies** for secondary-level Physics:

1. **Relate and Describe:** Relate the phenomena and processes of the world around them to the knowledge and understanding of physical laws, principles, and theories, and describe them using appropriate scientific vocabulary, terminology, and conventions.
2. **Measure and Communicate:** Use scientific instruments, apparatus, and methods to collect, evaluate, and communicate information accurately and precisely.
3. **Experiment and Analyze:** Design simple experiments to develop relationships among physical quantities and test hypotheses.
4. **Scientific Inquiry:** Carry out simple scientific research or investigations on issues related to physics.
5. **Model Construction:** Construct simple models to illustrate physical concepts and abstract theories.

### B. Physics Lab Synthesized Educational & Engineering Goals
The following objectives are pedagogical and technical enhancements formulated by the Physics Lab project for Shree Public High School, Dharan:

- **Interactive Dynamic Visualization:** Bridge abstract mathematical formulas with interactive Canvas/WebGL simulations navigable using a standard Android TV remote D-pad.
- **Pre-Laboratory Virtual Readiness:** Provide virtual equipment training (calipers, spherometer, potentiometer, spectrometer) to build student confidence and reduce apparatus damage prior to hands-on wet labs.
- **Classroom Smart TV Group Instruction:** Support front-of-class interactive teaching where teachers can adjust physical variables in real time and observe instantaneous changes in trajectories, vector fields, and plots.
- **Local Context Awareness:** Emphasize real-world physical systems in Nepal, including Himalayan seismic wave dynamics, hydroelectric generation at Upper Tamakoshi, highway curve banking in mountainous terrains, and solar off-grid energy storage.
- **Strict Offline Operation:** Deliver all curriculum simulations locally without requiring internet connectivity in rural or regional classroom environments.
